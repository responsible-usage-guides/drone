# Glossary

**Plain-language explanations of the drone and humanitarian terms used in this guide.**

---

## Drone terms

### AGL (above ground level)

Height measured from the ground directly below the drone, rather than from where it took off or from sea level. Keeping a constant AGL over hilly ground keeps the level of detail even.

### AOI (area of interest)

The area you want to map, usually drawn as a boundary on a map.

### Elevation model

A map of heights. A **surface model (DSM)** includes buildings and trees; a **terrain model (DTM)** shows the bare ground.

### Geofencing

Restrictions built into a drone's software by the manufacturer, which warn you or stop it flying in certain zones (such as near airports). Separate from government flight authorization.

### GCPs (ground control points)

Marked points on the ground whose exact location is measured with a precise GPS device. They're used to line up the final map accurately. Only needed when precise measurement matters, for example for 3D or elevation outputs.

### GeoTIFF

An image file that also stores where on Earth it is, so mapping software can place it correctly. The usual format for sharing finished drone maps. A **COG** (Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF) is a version that works well on the web.

### GSD (ground sampling distance)

How much ground one pixel in the image covers. A GSD of 4 cm means each pixel shows a 4 x 4 cm patch. Smaller numbers mean more detail.

### Gimbal

The motorized mount that holds the camera steady and controls which way it points.

### LiPo battery

Lithium polymer battery, the type used in most drones. Light and powerful, but a fire risk if damaged.

### NOTAM (Notice to Air Missions)

An official notice of a temporary airspace restriction or hazard, such as an emergency helicopter operation.

### Orthomosaic

A large, flat, top-down map image stitched together from many drone photos, and corrected so that distances can be measured on it.

### Overlap

How much each photo shares with its neighbours, along the flight line (front overlap) and between flight lines (side overlap). Processing needs plenty of overlap to stitch photos together.

### Photogrammetry

The process of turning many overlapping photos into maps and 3D models.

### Point cloud

A 3D model of an area made of millions of individual points, each with a position and colour. Usually shared as a **COPC** (Cloud Optimized Point Cloud) file, which, like a COG, can be viewed on the web without downloading the whole thing.

### RTH (Return to Home)

A safety feature that flies the drone back to its take-off point automatically, for example when the battery is low or the signal is lost.

### RTK / PPK

Ways of correcting a drone's GPS position to within a few centimetres, either during the flight (RTK) or afterwards (PPK). An alternative to ground control points.

### VLOS (visual line of sight)

Being able to see the drone directly with your own eyes, not only through the screen. Required in most countries. In practice, a small drone can be hard to pick out at mapping altitude, so keeping a clear view of the whole flight area matters as much as tracking the drone itself.

## Humanitarian terms

### Assessment working group

The group that coordinates needs assessments in a response, so organizations don't duplicate each other. Aerial assessment coordination usually sits within it.

### Information management working group

The group that coordinates how data and maps are collected, managed, and shared in a response.

### NDMA (national disaster management authority)

The government body responsible for disaster preparedness and response in a country. Names vary between countries.

### OCHA

The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs, which coordinates international humanitarian responses.

### UNDAC

The United Nations Disaster Assessment and Coordination team: experts deployed at short notice to help a government assess and coordinate the response to a sudden emergency.
